What to Do When Your Car Breaks Down in Miami

A breakdown on a busy Miami road can be stressful. Put safety first, describe your location clearly, and request the correct type of roadside help.

1. Move Away From Traffic When Possible

If the vehicle can move safely, guide it to a shoulder, parking lot, or other visible area away from active lanes. Turn on hazard lights. Do not attempt a risky move if steering, brakes, a wheel, smoke, fire, or traffic conditions make it unsafe.

2. Choose the Safest Place to Wait

Stay alert to traffic and follow guidance from emergency authorities. If there is a collision, injury, fire, fuel leak, immediate danger, or blocked travel lane, contact 911. Do not stand between vehicles or beside fast-moving traffic.

3. Send Dispatch Exact Details

  • Road name, direction, nearest intersection, exit, landmark, or shared location pin.
  • Vehicle year, make, model, color, and plate when available.
  • Whether the car is in a lane, shoulder, garage, lot, or restricted area.
  • The problem: no start, flat tire, overheating, lockout, collision, or mechanical failure.
  • Any special access, low clearance, damage, or safety concern.

4. Request the Right Service

A jump start, lockout, tire issue, or fuel problem may need roadside assistance. A damaged, overheating, non-running, or unsafe vehicle may need towing or flatbed transport. Clear details help dispatch choose suitable equipment.
